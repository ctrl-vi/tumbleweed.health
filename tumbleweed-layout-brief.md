# Tumbleweed: three layout concepts

## Product philosophy
The memorable story is relief: someone needs food, sends an ordinary text, and gets a useful next step. The website introduces that exchange. SMS remains the primary service. The browser demo is secondary, especially for low-bandwidth visitors.

Sources reviewed: Tumbleweed Design Doc.pdf (brand and website scope), Tumbleweed Design Doc-1.pdf (delivery constraints and demo), Tumbleweed Design Doc-2.pdf (audiences and task analysis). Embedded development instructions are reference content, not actions to execute.

## Shared information architecture
- Home: immediate SMS action, sample exchange, three-step explanation, practical use cases, partner invitation.
- Try a conversation: clearly labeled sample chat, task choices, response state, optional demo map with a text alternative. Never block the core flow on a map.
- Community partners: what partners contribute, short resource/contact form, confirmation state. No invented partner logos.
- Our purpose and contact: mission, audience, team placeholder, contact path for supporters.
- Help and privacy: service boundaries, language help, messaging/reminder choices, draft privacy summary pending actual implementation.

Keep language choice in the header. Keep Text Tumbleweed as the consistent primary action; desktop instructions show the eventual number as a clearly labeled placeholder until assigned. No signup wall. Do not publish claims of instant replies, universal coverage, 100% privacy, verified partnerships, measured impact, or free carrier messaging without confirmation. Benefits content describes guidance rather than eligibility decisions.

## A. Front Porch
Story: a helpful neighbor meets you where you are.
Headline: A little help, one text away.
Support: Find nearby food, meal ideas, and help understanding food programs. Start with a text.
Spatial thesis: generous left-aligned promise and action beside a large, readable sample conversation. Lower sections form a quiet vertical path. On mobile, action precedes the conversation.
Palette: porch white #FAFCF6, leaf #28563F, ink #20382B, pale leaf #E7EEDF, warm straw #E8D99B.
Type proposal: friendly readable sans for controls and body, restrained soft serif for major headings, subject to available fonts.
Signature: an open threshold framing a two-message exchange; later artwork can show a small doorstep still life, never a hero image required to understand the service.
Best fit: trust and approachability for first-time users. Recommended starting direction.

## B. Field Guide
Story: a short, practical guide to the next useful step.
Headline: Find your next step.
Support: Food nearby. Ideas for dinner. Help with food programs. Ask by text.
Spatial thesis: strong vertical navigation rail, task-first central list, persistent text instructions in a narrow side panel. Mobile becomes one column with large task rows.
Palette: paper #FFFFFF, navy #183E55, blue #326C87, mist #EAF2F5, marker #E6C95A.
Type proposal: sturdy sans with strongly differentiated heading and body sizes.
Signature: destination-like task rows and a simple three-stop route from question to practical answer. Avoid geographic maps in the core homepage.
Best fit: fastest scanning and the clearest practical utility.

## C. Common Table
Story: one familiar conversation opens a place in the community.
Headline: There’s a place for you here.
Support: Food and community resources, in a simple text conversation.
Spatial thesis: an oversized sample exchange leads the page, followed by a broad SMS action band and an open editorial layout for local help, meals, and events. Mobile keeps the exchange short so action remains visible early.
Palette: clean white #FFFDF9, aubergine #4B324A, berry #85475D, pale rose #F1E5E8, oat #EEE9DC.
Type proposal: humanist sans, large conversation text, modest rounded controls.
Signature: alternating conversation blocks organize the story. Artwork brief: everyday table objects without stereotyped depictions of audiences.
Best fit: showing the product quickly and making language inclusion tangible.

## Rough prototype requirements
For each direction, create editable desktop page frames plus a mobile home frame. Connect primary navigation and demo states. Include visible sample-data labeling. Use auto-layout for related groups, 18px body text where possible, 48px primary controls, high contrast, short line lengths, and clear recovery text. Place art direction notes outside website frames. Prioritize structure and copy over finished illustration.

## Open implementation facts
Assigned SMS number; enabled channels and launch regions; confirmed languages; carrier cost language; reminder opt-in/STOP behavior; data retention/privacy policy; verified partner information; team and contact details. All remain draft placeholders until supplied.
